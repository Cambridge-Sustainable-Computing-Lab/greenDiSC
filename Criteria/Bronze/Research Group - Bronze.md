## Appoint a Green DiSC Representative

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-general-blue)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RG)%20G1--B-white)

### Impact
Ensures someone takes the lead on digital sustainability.

### Description
Continuity is crucial to all sustainability initiatives and is the sign of a truly successful effort. This ensures that someone is nominated (and a replacement is identified if they leave) to carry forward the targets laid out in this framework. Providing recognition for the work done in this space is also key.

### How?
A member of staff takes on the responsibility and makes sure to pass it on when they leave. They also provide a link between the team and the central sustainability/IT offices. Their role should be clearly signposted in relevant institutional profile pages and role descriptions. Such responsibility should also bring recognition to the staff member(s) taking it on (e.g. during promotion and appraisal).

### Checklist
1) Nominate a Green DiSC representative from your group and ensure they understand the responsibilities involved
2) Document their nomination as the Green DiSC representative (e.g. include in job description, institutional profile page)
  - Include their contact details in this documentation (e.g. institutional email)
3) Highlight how this is recognised within the organisation (optional)

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That a Green DiSC representative has been nominated and that their role and responsibilities are well-documented within the organisation. 
- That staff members can easily identify who this representative is.
- That you have thought about how this work is being recognised internally.

**Evidence can include:**
- Screenshot of their institutional profile page highlighting their role as Green DiSC representative.
- Screenshot of their role description in group/institutional documentation.
- Updated role description for the staff member.
- List of ways of how this is being recognised within the team and within the organisation.

## Include digital sustainability in induction procedures

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-general-blue)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RG)%20G2--B-white)

### Impact
Emphasises the importance of adopting efficient and sustainable practices from day one.

### Description
While there may be a substantial amount of information for new starters, it is crucial that efficient and sustainable computing practices are emphasised on arrival, when new staff behaviour can be most influenced.

### How?
Sustainability induction procedures can be developed by the institution's central sustainability team, or used just for your team and inspired from examples in other institutions. Sustainable computing advice should not be separate but rather fully integrated into induction materials.

This criteria refers to training about digital and computing sustainability, not generic campus sustainability efforts.

*📣 Ask your Central Team! If your central team has applied or is applying to Green DiSC as well, you should be able to ask them about the evidence they provided for this criterion and what relevant information is available for you to include in your application.*

### Checklist
1) Identify suitable induction material applicable to your team
  - Search on your institution’s intranet or contact your central sustainability team if appropriate
  - You can also consult online resources shared on the [ESCS forum](http://forum.escs-community.org) or listed above. 
2) Include material covering digital sustainability in induction pack
  - Detail how this material is disseminated and how it is made accessible to new starters (including how many have received it already)

### Resources and Case Studies
- [Sustainable computing in science course](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/sustainable-computing-in-science/)
- [Green Software Practitioner Course](https://learn.greensoftware.foundation/)
- [Green HPC system use](https://epcced.github.io/2025-07-02_GreenHPC_UCL/)

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- What induction material is used with new starters (including where it comes from, e.g. created from scratch or built from existing material).
- How is it being shared, and how many staff members have seen it.

**Evidence can include:**
- Copy of the induction material* (e.g. PDF, docx, pptx).
- Communication (e.g. photos, screenshot, PDF, emails) showing how the induction material is shared with new starters.

*You may use discretion to redact any information you deem confidential/sensitive, as long as you are still able to show the relevant evidence for the purpose of fulfilling the action.

## Share resources with other groups

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-general-blue)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RG)%20G3--B-white)

### Impact
Increases adoption, strengthens sustainability initiatives, and avoids duplicating efforts.

### Description
Information collected that could be useful to other groups in the institution (e.g. data centres specifications, induction material) are shared across the institution. Material created or improved (e.g. induction or training) is also shared externally on the [ESCS Forum](https://forum.escs-community.org/c/green-disc/9).

### How?
Internally, information that could be useful to other groups doing Green DiSC is shared in some central repository (this can be coordinated by the central sustainability team), e.g. the institution's intranet.

Externally, relevant resources are shared on the [ESCS Forum](https://forum.escs-community.org/c/green-disc/9).

### Checklist
1) Identify what resources should be shared internally and externally.
  - Identify the best way to share them internally in your institution.
2) Share the resources internally and externally.
  - Document who this is shared with.

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That the relevant resources have been identified and shared, both internally and externally.
- What resources are being shared.
- Who these resources have been shared with internally.

**Evidence can include:**
- Screenshots of the resources shared on an intranet or shared repository.
- Photos or screenshots of the sharing permissions of the resources.
- Communications of these resources being shared with other groups.
- Evidence of other groups using these resources.
- Link to the forum's post sharing relevant resources (if any).

## Disseminate this program in the institution

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-general-blue)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RG)%20G4--B-white)

### Impact
Increases participation, adoption of sustainability initiatives, and favours collaboration.

### Description
Aside from shared resources, sharing experiences, tips, and best practices with other groups encourages engagement and helps grow and strengthen a community within your institution that is dedicated to sustainability. Encouraging other groups to participate is also a great way to reduce the environmental impacts of the institution as a whole.

### How?
You first identify suitable channels of communication within the institution, primarily by linking with the central sustainability team and other sustainability networks (e.g. mailing lists, research students). Then you can share your experience and encourage other groups to participate.

### Checklist
1) Identify the best communication channels, and use them to encourage participation.
  - Document how your group engages with other groups to disseminate this program.
2) Provide evidence of these promotion activities.

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- Which suitable communication channels have been identified.
- How these were used.
- What effect it's had on other groups in your institution.

**Evidence can include:**
- List of communication channels identified.
- Screenshots of communication done: e.g. online announcements, emails, and other forms of communication with other groups.
- Example of impact of this dissemination.

## Catalog the office IT equipment

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-offices-yellow)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RG)%2001--B-white)

### Impact
Enables the team to monitor the equipment owned, ensuring that none gets forgotten about.

### Description
Create an inventory of the different computing hardware used by the group in the office (not personal equipment), whether or not it is currently in use, as well as suppliers and warranty policies that apply. This list is updated yearly and includes laptops, desktop computers, monitors, tablets, and external hard drives. This should not include HPC equipment, as this is a separate criteria.

*Why? Embodied carbon, or the emissions associated with the manufacturing, transportation, installation, maintenance, and end-of-life stages of a product, can take up a significant portion of emissions for equipment. For desktops and laptops, manufacturing takes up about 80% of the total life cycle emissions of the product – greatly outweighing the environmental impacts of the usage phase. Keeping track of equipment is a good way to limit how much new hardware is bought.*

### How?
List the IT equipment owned by the group by linking with colleagues (e.g. circulate a survey). Where data is missing, this should be clearly highlighted so that it can be revisited in the future. In the case where a personal device is used significantly for work (e.g. using your own laptop), then this may be included for monitoring purposes. Unused equipment (e.g. spare or unusable equipment or parts) should also be listed and identified as such. Include information on actions to be taken for each, and information on how to interact with asset management systems, if available.

### Checklist
1) Inventory the relevant computing hardware equipment used by each member of your group
  - Laptops (i.e. brand, model, general specs)
  - External monitors (e.g. size, specs)
  - External hard drives (e.g. SSD, HDD)
2) For each, identify quantity, owner etc.
  - Indicate the quantity per item
  - Indicate the owner
  - Indicate if it’s currently in use
  - (Optional) Indicate the location, if office/storage is spread out

### Resources and Case Studies
- You may use [this template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1GEUPeHO6CnJhCOhJHdSKYH6c0cKhPyXy3UUZzWpv7WI/edit?gid=0#gid=0) created by a previous awardee as reference, just download a copy if you would like to adopt the table(s).

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That the inventory has been put together and covers the entire group.
- That enough data is included in the inventory and gaps are clearly identified.
- That unused hardware is clearly identified.

**Evidence can include:**
- The filled inventory with the different elements listed in the checklist.

## Document the "Reuse, Repair, Recycle" process for IT equipment

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-offices-yellow)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RG)%2002--B-white)

### Impact
Understand what your team’s and institution's policies are for minimising and recycling e-waste from your team.

### Description
There is documentation of how broken, spare, and unusable equipment (i.e. electronic waste) from the group is dealt with. As much as possible, broken equipment is repaired, spare equipment is reallocated, and any electronic waste is recycled. 

*Why? Non-recycled e-waste is a rapidly growing issue across the world, and electronic devices piling up in dump sites – often in low income countries – are responsible for serious health consequences.*

### How?
List the disposing streams available from institution guidelines, as well as any more specific best-practices implemented by your team. These processes can include internal reuse, external reuse, recycling, resale through external vendors, and processing any electronic waste through take-back schemes.

*📣 Ask your Central Team! If your central team has applied or is applying to Green DiSC as well, you should be able to ask them about the evidence they provided for this criterion and what relevant information is available for you to include in your application.*

### Checklist
1) List the different processes in place to Reuse, Repair, Recycle electronic hardware
2) Ensure your group is complying with them and communicate them with your teammates

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That you have a list of the different processes in place to "Reuse, Repair, Recycle" your computing hardware.
- That these have been communicated to your entire team.
- That these processes are followed.

**Evidence can include:**
- Details of the different processes in place, with explanation of how it's implemented in practice in your group.
- Proof of communication of these processes within the team.

## Catalog the major data resources used

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-data%20storage-red)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RG)%20DS1--B-white)

### Impact
Allows the group to track the major data resources used and facilitates sustainable data management. 

### Description
There is an inventory of the major data resources used by the group (whether directly managed by the team or not), including key information about each dataset (storage location, number of users, size, whether the data is unique or duplicate of another repository, number of backups, etc.). This inventory is updated regularly (frequency depends on field, at least yearly).

### How?
List the major data resources by linking with colleagues and confirming with IT teams. “Major”refers to significance in terms of size or difficulty to replace. Include any available information: storage location, number of users, size, whether the data is unique or duplicate of another repository, number of backups, etc. This includes both data stored on your own servers and external data you rely on (for these, you may not be able to obtain all the information above, but as much details as possible should be included).

### Checklist
1) Link with the rest of your group to create an inventory of all the main data resources
For each, add the following elements (if needed, by linking with the IT team):
  - Name
  - Storage location
  - Number of users
  - Size
  - Is the data unique or duplicated from elsewhere
  - Back-up location

### Resources and Case Studies
You may use [this template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1GEUPeHO6CnJhCOhJHdSKYH6c0cKhPyXy3UUZzWpv7WI/edit?gid=1362671896#gid=1362671896) created by a previous awardee as reference, just download a copy if you would like to adopt the table(s).

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That the inventory has been put together and covers all the major datasets.
- That each data resource is described clearly and concisely.

**Evidence can include:**
- The filled inventory with the different elements listed in the checklist.

## Start a data cleaning process

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-data%20storage-red)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RG)%20DS2--B-white)

### Impact
Avoids unnecessary accumulation of data that is no longer needed.

### Description
There is a clear and regularly occurring process (twice a year is ideal) for the cleaning of data directories that are used and maintained by users of the group. This process should include regular reminders for deletion of temporary/outdated files.

### How?
Organise regular cleaning in coordination with central IT teams and users.

*📣 Ask your Central Team! If your central team has applied or is applying to Green DiSC as well, you should be able to ask them about the data cleaning process they provided for this criterion and what relevant information is available for you to include in your application.*

### Checklist
1) Document the data cleaning process and its frequency
2) Implement it and document when it's happened which repositories were included, who took part, and how much space was freed
  - Include detail on the frequency and next scheduled cleaning.

### References
- [Eleven quick tips for organizing a data cleaning challenge](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013791)

### Resources and Case Studies
- For best practice, you can use [this resource as a DMP template](https://dmponline.dcc.ac.uk), but there are many other options available online.

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That there is a clear process for data cleaning.
- That it's been implemented, and what its effect was (i.e. describe the type of data cleared and size of storage freed).

**Evidence can include:**
- The data cleaning policy and where it is located.
- Screenshots of communications to organise data cleaning and showing when the reminders are sent out.
- Documentation of effects of the data cleaning event.

## Catalog computing infrastructures used

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-compute-silver)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RG)%20C1--B-white)

### Impact
Provides an exhaustive list of the current services used by the group and the sustainability credentials of each.

### Description
There is a list of the computing infrastructures (i.e. buildings, data centres) used by the group, including both on-site (e.g. local or institutional data centres, servers in the office) and off-site (e.g. remote institutional computing facilities and commercial cloud providers). This inventory is updated regularly (frequency depends on field, at least yearly). It is recommended to coordinate with any central IT teams and the IT estate to ensure that this list is comprehensive. Any infrastructure that the group is using (or can be reasonably expected to use at some point) should be included.

### How?
This inventory should at least include, for each on-site data centre: location, Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE), cooling technology used, and information on the electricity sources of each infrastructure (i.e. either main power grid, most common, or dedicated power source such as a solar farm). Power purchase agreements can be mentioned, but don’t replace listing the primary source. Noteworthy energy efficiency technology implemented can be included in this inventory. 

For cloud providers: company providing the service, location of the data centres, PUE and hardware if you can find it.

If you’re using your own computing infrastructure, e.g. servers in the office, the reason for doing so should be stated.

*📣 Share your response with your Research Computing Infrastructure team! If you have RCI team/s from your institution also enrolled in Green DiSC, they may reach out to you about the evidence you provide in this criterion to help them with their application.*

### Checklist
1) Create a list of computing infrastructures with appropriate details (listed above), both for on-site and off-site data centres

### References
- You may use [this template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1GEUPeHO6CnJhCOhJHdSKYH6c0cKhPyXy3UUZzWpv7WI/edit?gid=1412730163#gid=1412730163) created by a previous awardee as reference, just download a copy if you would like to adopt the table(s).

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That the inventory has been put together and covers all the data centres and computing infrastructures used by your group.
- That on-site data centres have all the information needed about them to understand their environmental impacts. 
- That off-site (e.g. cloud) data centres are also listed with as much information as possible.
- That gaps are clearly identified.

**Evidence can include:**
- The filled inventory with the different elements listed in this Action.

## Identify computing workflow hotspots

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-compute-silver)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RG)%20C2--B-white)

### Impact
Understanding the current computing pipelines run by the group enables long-term improvements and carbon accounting, and supports sustainability policies.

### Description
There is a list of the major computing workflows run by the group: by "major" we mean the ones that are the most demanding (if any), either because of long runtimes or large number of resources, or because the same pipeline is run a large number of times.

### How?
By linking with colleagues and IT teams as necessary, you can identify the main workflows and create an inventory of them, with details about each one (e.g. users, runtimes, resource used, frequency, software it depends on, which data centre it runs on). 

### Checklist
1) List the most demanding computing workflows ran by the group
  - Include detail on metric used for demand (e.g. runtime, number of cores and memory, number times run)

### Resources and Case Studies
- You may use [this template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1GEUPeHO6CnJhCOhJHdSKYH6c0cKhPyXy3UUZzWpv7WI/edit?gid=1945516641#gid=1945516641) created by a previous awardee as reference, just download a copy if you would like to adopt the table(s).

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That you have built a comprehensive inventory of the most demanding computing workflows.
- That you have collected sufficient details about each of them.
- That you have a process to keep this up-to-date.

**Evidence can include:**
- The filled inventory with the different elements listed above.

## Collect educational material on sustainable computing best practices

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-compute-silver)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RG)%20C3--B-white)

### Impact
Raises awareness, educates, and establishes a standard for the group to follow.

### Description
The group raises awareness, educates, and shares information on green computing best practices with its team members, and documents tips that are relevant to the group’s work. This can include reducing re-run analysis when not necessary, how to debug on a small scale and use checkpointing to limit unnecessary runs, and known coding optimisation best practices relevant to the group's activities.

### How?
The group should collect key information on green computing that is particularly relevant to its activities. You can leverage already available training material online (e.g. [Sustainable computing in science course](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/sustainable-computing-in-science/)), or design their own. The [ESCS Forum](https://forum.escs-community.org) is also a great resource for this. 

There should be a process in place to make sure all team members are upskilled in this domain.

*📣 Ask your Central Team! If your central team has applied or is applying to Green DiSC as well, you should be able to ask them about the evidence they provided for this criterion and what relevant information is available for you to include in your application.*

### Checklist
1) Identify suitable resources (internal and external) that apply to your circumstances
  - List the green computing-related training resources offered
  - Provide detail on how these are disseminated and made accessible
2) Encourage team members to engage with these
  - Include detail on the date and attendance list of the training sessions(s)
  - Provide any documents used for training (including tips that are specific to the lab)
  - Highlight how this is made available to all group members.

### Resources and Case Studies
You can use some of these online on-demand training courses: the [SPARKhub Training: Emissions of Digital Research Infrastructure](https://sdratraining.sparkhub.eu/) which is quite short, or [EMBL-EBI’s Sustainable computing in science](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/sustainable-computing-in-science/) training for a slightly deeper deep-dive.

You may use this [template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1GEUPeHO6CnJhCOhJHdSKYH6c0cKhPyXy3UUZzWpv7WI/edit?gid=891076769#gid=891076769) created by a previous awardee as reference, just download a copy if you would like to adopt the table(s).

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That you have a policy in place to promote green computing best practices in your group.
- What resources and advice are made available to group members, and tailored to the activities of your group.
- That team members have been actively encouraged to upskill. 

**Evidence can include:**
- Screenshot of shared repository listing the group-specific green computing policy.
- List of content of this repository, with screenshots.
- Description of how these are disseminated and made accessible.
- Date and attendance for any training session/s conducted.
