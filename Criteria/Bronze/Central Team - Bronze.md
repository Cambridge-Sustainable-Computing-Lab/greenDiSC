## Appoint a Green DiSC Representative

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-general-blue)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(CT)%20G1--B-white)

### Impact
Ensures someone takes the lead on digital sustainability.

### Description
Continuity is crucial to all sustainability initiatives and is the sign of a truly successful effort. This ensures that someone is nominated (and a replacement is identified if they leave) to carry forward the targets laid out in this framework. Providing recognition for the work done in this space is also key.

### How?
A member of staff takes on the responsibility and makes sure to pass it on when they leave. They also provide a link between the team and research computing infrastructure teams and research groups. Their role should be clearly signposted in relevant institutional profile pages and role descriptions. Such responsibility should also bring recognition to the staff member(s) taking it on (e.g. during promotion and appraisal).

### Checklist
1) Nominate a Green DiSC representative from your team and ensure they understand the responsibilities involved
2) Document their nomination as the Green DiSC representative (e.g. include in job description, institutional profile page)
  - Include their contact details in this documentation (e.g. institutional email)
3) Highlight how this is recognised within the organisation (optional)

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That a Green DiSC representative has been nominated and that their role and responsibilities are well-documented within the organisation. 
- That staff members can easily identify who this representative is.
- That you have thought about how this work is being recognised internally.

**Evidence can include:**
- Screenshot of their institutional profile page highlighting their role as Green DiSC representative.
Screenshot of their role description in group/institutional documentation.
- Updated role description for the staff member.
- List of ways of how this is being recognised within the team and within the organisation.

## Support the inclusion of digital sustainability in induction procedures

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-general-blue)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(CT)%20G2--B-white)

### Impact
Emphasises the importance of adopting efficient and sustainable practices from day one.

### Description
While there may be a substantial amount of information for new starters, it is crucial that efficient and sustainable computing practices are emphasised on arrival, when new staff behaviour can be most influenced.

### How?
Sustainability induction procedures can be developed in-house by your team or inspired from examples in other institutions. Sustainable computing advice should not be separate but rather fully integrated into induction materials.

This criteria refers to training about digital and computing sustainability, not generic campus sustainability efforts. You should make some template available to research groups across your organisation and if possible, embed these in default induction material circulated centrally.

*📣 Share your response with your groups! If there are other research groups from your institution also enrolled in Green DiSC and you help facilitate their work, they may reach out to you about the evidence you provide in this criterion to help them with their application.*

### Checklist
1) Identify suitable induction material
- You can also consult online resources shared on the [ESCS forum](http://forum.escs-community.org) or listed above
2) Include material covering digital sustainability in induction pack
- Detail how this material is disseminated and how it is made accessible to new starters (including how many have received it already)

### Resources and Case Studies
- [Sustainable Computing in Science course (EMBL-EBI)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/sustainable-computing-in-science/)
- [Green Software Practitioner Course](https://learn.greensoftware.foundation)
- [Green HPC System Course (EPCC/Carpentries)](https://epcced.github.io/2025-07-02_GreenHPC_UCL/)

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- What induction material is made available to groups for use with new starters (including where it comes from, e.g. created from scratch or built from existing material).
- How is it being shared, and how many staff members have seen it.

**Evidence can include:**
- Copy of the induction material* (e.g. PDF, docx, pptx).
- Communication (e.g. photos, screenshot, PDF, emails) showing how the induction material is shared with new starters.

*You may use discretion to redact any information you deem confidential/sensitive, as long as you are still able to show the relevant evidence for the purpose of fulfilling the action.

## Centralise sustainable computing information in a repository

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-general-blue)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(CT)%20G3--B-white)

### Impact
Helps establish common knowledge across teams and ensures resources are stored centrally and easily accessible.

### Description
Sustainability materials and resources are centrally located in a repository that is made available to groups across the institutions. Material that is created from scratch or improved from pre-existing material (e.g. induction or training) is shared externally on the [Green DiSC forum](https://forum.escs-community.org/c/green-disc/9).

### How?
Establish a central repository dedicated to storing sustainable computing resources, and ensure this repository is accessible to users and disseminated effectively. Resources stored here should be relevant to research activities done by groups supported by the central team, rather than general, operational sustainability resources.

### Checklist
1) Establish a central repository of sustainable computing resources
2) Ensure this repository is disseminated to users and that it is easily accessible

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That a central repository is established and stored relevant sustainable computing resources, useful to research groups supported by the central team.
- That this repository is effectively disseminated by the team and it is accessible for users.

**Evidence can include:**
- Screenshot of the central repository and resources stored within.
- Screenshot of dissemination activities (e.g. emails, announcements) and access permissions.

## Disseminate this program in the institution

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-general-blue)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(CT)%20G4--B-white)

### Impact
Increases participation, adoption of sustainability initiatives, and favours collaboration.

### Description
Aside from shared resources, sharing experiences, tips, and best practices encourages engagement and helps grow and strengthen a community within your institution that is dedicated to sustainability. Encouraging research groups to participate is also a great way to reduce the environmental impacts of the institution as a whole.

### How?
You can identify suitable channels of communication within the institution, such as through sustainability networks (e.g. mailing lists, research students) or contact with departments. You can share your experience, highlight how the central team can help, and encourage other groups to participate.

### Checklist
1) Identify the best communication channels, and use them to encourage participation.
  - Document how your group engages with other groups to disseminate this program.
2) Provide evidence of these promotion activities.

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- Which suitable communication channels have been identified.
- How these were used.
- What effect it's had on other teams in your institution.

**Evidence can include:**
- List of communication channels identified.
- Screenshots of communication done: e.g. online announcements, emails, and other forms of communication with other groups.
- Example of impact of this dissemination.

## Catalog purchasing streams for office IT equipment

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-offices-yellow)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(CT)%2001--B-white)

### Impact
Enables groups to monitor how hardware is being purchased, allowing for sustainability improvements in procurement processes.

### Description
Create an inventory of the different purchasing streams across the institution for acquiring computing hardware, their respective suppliers, and the warranty policies that apply. This list is updated yearly.

*Why? Embodied carbon, or the emissions associated with the manufacturing, transportation, installation, maintenance, and end-of-life stages of a product, can take up a significant portion of emissions for equipment. For desktops and laptops, manufacturing takes up about 80% of the total life cycle emissions of the product – greatly outweighing the environmental impacts of the usage phase.*

### How?
List the purchasing streams for computing hardware, citing the suppliers and corresponding warranty policies. This scope covers laptops, desktop computers, monitors, tablets, and external hard drives. This should not include HPC equipment, as this is a separate criteria.

### Checklist
1) Inventory the purchasing streams for computing hardware
  - Purchasing process
  - Suppliers
  - Warranty policies

### Resources and Case Studies
You may use [this template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1GEUPeHO6CnJhCOhJHdSKYH6c0cKhPyXy3UUZzWpv7WI/edit?gid=1362671896#gid=1362671896) created by a previous awardee as reference, just download a copy if you would like to adopt the table(s).

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That the inventory has been put together and covers purchasing streams for computing hardware.
- That supporting information is provided (e.g. purchasing process, suppliers, warranty policies).

**Evidence can include:**
- The filled inventory with the different purchasing streams listed.

## Document the “Reuse, Repair, Recycle” process for IT equipment

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-offices-yellow)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(CT)%2002--B-white)

### Impact
Understand and communicate what your institution's policies are for minimising and recycling e-waste from your team.

### Description
There is documentation of how broken, spare, and unusable equipment (i.e. electronic waste). As much as possible, broken equipment is repaired, spare equipment is reallocated, and any electronic waste is recycled.

*Why? Non-recycled e-waste is a rapidly growing issue across the world, and electronic devices piling up in dump sites – often in low income countries – are responsible for serious health consequences.*

### How?
List the disposing streams available from institution guidelines. These processes can include internal reuse, external reuse, recycling, resale through external vendors, and processing any electronic waste through take-back schemes.

*📣 Share your response with your groups! If there are research groups from your institution also enrolled in Green DiSC and you help facilitate their work, they may reach out to you about the evidence you provide in this criterion to help them with their application.*

### Checklist
1) List the different processes in place to Reuse, Repair, Recycle electronic hardware
2) Ensure your team is complying with them and communicate them with your teammates

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That you have a list of the different processes in place to "Reuse, Repair, Recycle" your computing hardware.
- That these have been communicated to your entire team.
- That these processes are followed.

**Evidence can include:**
- Details of the different processes in place, with explanation of how it's implemented in practice in your team.
- Proof of communication of these processes within the team.

## Catalog data storage solutions

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-data%20storage-red)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(CT)%20DS1--B-white)

### Impact
Allows the teams to monitor how data storage is used, enabling more efficient use and reducing clutter, as it can be a significant contributor to research energy usage.

### Description
There is a list of data storage options available to the institution and separate research groups, with information on available options for (large) data storage and, in particular, which are user’s responsibility versus which are centrally managed. User-responsible data storage could be local drives on computers and hard drives, while centrally managed data storage can include cloud storage and shared drives.

### How?
By linking with IT teams, RCI teams, and research groups within your organisation, list the different options of data storage options available. Include any supporting detail, such as capacity, usage, location, data types stored, etc.

### Checklist
1) Link with other teams and research groups to create an inventory of your largest data storage solutions available
For each, add the following elements (if needed, by linking with the IT team):
  - Name
  - Storage location
  - Capacity
  - Usage
  - Data types stored

### Resources and Case Studies
You may use [this template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1GEUPeHO6CnJhCOhJHdSKYH6c0cKhPyXy3UUZzWpv7WI/edit?gid=1362671896#gid=1362671896) created by a previous awardee as reference, just download a copy if you would like to adopt the table(s).

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That an inventory has been put together and cover all of the largest data storage solutions.
- That each storage solution is described clearly and concisely.

**Evidence can include:**
- The filled inventory with supporting detail.

## Develop a data cleaning process for research groups to adopt

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-data%20storage-red)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(CT)%20DS2--B-white)

### Impact
Avoids unnecessary accumulation of data that is no longer needed.

### Description
There is a template for the data cleaning process that research groups can adopt to regularly clean their data directories. 

### How?
Have a process in place to organise regular cleaning designed to be adopted by research groups.

*📣 Share your response with your groups! If there are other research groups from your institution also enrolled in Green DiSC and you help facilitate their work, they may reach out to you about the evidence you provide in this criterion to help them with their application.*

### Checklist
1) Document the data cleaning process and its recommended frequency
2) Document how it has been disseminated, and include detail on the next scheduled cleaning.
3) If it has already been adopted, detail which groups took part, when it occurred, and how much space was freed (optional)

### References
- [Eleven quick tips for organizing a data cleaning challenge](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013791)

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That there is a clear process for data cleaning designed for research groups to adopt.
- That it's been disseminated to research groups, and what its effect was (i.e. describe the groups involved, if it has been adopted and by which groups).

**Evidence can include:**
- The data cleaning policy and where it is located.
- Screenshots of communications to organise data cleaning and showing when the reminders are sent out to research groups.
- Documentation of effects of the data cleaning event (e.g. type of data cleared, and size of storage freed).

Helps optimise and reduce research energy usage from storage.

### Description
There are templates and training resources for data management that are made available to research groups and other teams in the institution. These resources should help guide users to structure practical processes for data collection, organisation, and use that fit their research operation needs.

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That you have relevant data management training resources and they are stored in a central repository.

## Catalog computing infrastructures available

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-compute-silver)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(CT)%20C1--B-white)

### Impact
Provides a broad list of the current services available and the sustainability credentials of each.

### Description
There is a list of the computing infrastructures available, including both on-site (e.g. local or institutional data centres, servers in the offices) and off-site (e.g. remote institutional computing facilities and commercial cloud providers). This inventory is updated regularly (frequency depends on institutions, at least yearly). It is recommended to coordinate with any central IT teams and the IT estate to ensure that this list is comprehensive.

### How?
This inventory should at least include, for each on-site data centre: location, Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE), cooling technology used, and information on the electricity sources of each infrastructure (i.e. either main power grid, most common, or dedicated power source such as a solar farm). Power purchase agreements can be mentioned, but don’t replace listing the primary source. Noteworthy energy efficiency technology implemented can be included in this inventory. 

For cloud providers: company providing the service, location of the data centres, PUE and hardware if you can find it.

*📣 Share your response with your Research Computing Infrastructure team! If you have RCI team/s from your institution also enrolled in Green DiSC, they may reach out to you about the evidence you provide in this criterion to help them with their application.*

### Checklist
1) Create a list of computing infrastructures with appropriate details (listed above), both for on-site and off-site data centres

### References
You may use [this template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1GEUPeHO6CnJhCOhJHdSKYH6c0cKhPyXy3UUZzWpv7WI/edit?gid=1412730163#gid=1412730163) created by a previous awardee as reference, just download a copy if you would like to adopt the table(s).

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That the inventory has been put together and covers all the data centres and computing infrastructures available.
- That on-site data centres have all the information needed about them to understand their environmental impacts. 
- That off-site (e.g. cloud) data centres are also listed with as much information as possible.
- That gaps are clearly identified.

**Evidence can include:**
- The filled inventory with the different elements listed in this Action.

## Collect educational material on sustainable computing best practices

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-compute-silver)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(CT)%20C2--B-white)

### Impact
Raises awareness, educates, and establishes a standard for supported groups to follow.

### Description
The team raises awareness, educates, and shares information on green computing best practices with the research groups in the organisation, and documents tips that are relevant to the groups’ research work. This can include reducing re-run analysis when not necessary, how to debug on a small scale and use checkpointing to limit unnecessary runs, and known coding optimisation best practices relevant to the group's activities.

### How?
The team should collect key information on green computing that is particularly relevant to its groups’ activities. You can leverage already available training material online (e.g. [Sustainable computing in science course](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/sustainable-computing-in-science/)), or design their own. The [ESCS Forum](https://forum.escs-community.org) is also a great resource for this. 

There should be a process in place to make sure research groups are upskilled in this domain.

*📣 Share your response with your groups! If there are other research groups from your institution also enrolled in Green DiSC and you help facilitate their work, they may reach out to you about the evidence you provide in this criterion to help them with their application.*

### Checklist
1) Identify suitable resources (internal and external) that apply to your circumstances
  - List the green computing-related training resources offered
  - Provide detail on how these are disseminated and made accessible
2) Encourage research groups to engage with these
  - Include detail on the date and attendance list of the training sessions(s)
  - Provide any documents used for training (including tips that are specific to the lab)
  - Highlight how this is made available to all research groups.

### Resources and Case Studies
You can use some of these online on-demand training courses: the [SPARKhub Training: Emissions of Digital Research Infrastructure](https://sdratraining.sparkhub.eu/) which is quite short, or [EMBL-EBI’s Sustainable computing in science](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/sustainable-computing-in-science/) training for a slightly deeper deep-dive.

You may use [this template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1GEUPeHO6CnJhCOhJHdSKYH6c0cKhPyXy3UUZzWpv7WI/edit?gid=891076769#gid=891076769) created by a previous awardee as reference, just download a copy if you would like to adopt the table(s).

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That you have a policy in place to promote green computing best practices in your institution.
- What resources and advice are made available to groups, and tailored to the activities of your team and research groups.
- That research groups have been actively encouraged to upskill. 

**Evidence can include:**
- Screenshot of shared repository listing the team-specific green computing policy.
- List of content of this repository, with screenshots.
- Description of how these are disseminated and made accessible.
- Date and attendance for any training session/s conducted.
