## Appoint a Green DiSC Representative

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-general-blue)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RCI)%20G1--B-white)

### Impact
Ensures someone takes the lead on digital sustainability.

### Description
Continuity is crucial to all sustainability initiatives and is the sign of a truly successful effort. This ensures that someone is nominated (and a replacement is identified if they leave) to carry forward the targets laid out in this framework. Providing recognition for the work done in this space is also key.

### How?
A member of the RCI team takes on the responsibility and makes sure to pass it on when they leave. They also provide a link between the team and the central sustainability/IT offices. Their role should be clearly signposted in relevant institutional profile pages and role descriptions. Such responsibility should also bring recognition to the staff member(s) taking it on (e.g. during promotion and appraisal).

### Checklist
1) Nominate a Green DiSC representative from your team and ensure they understand the responsibilities involved
2) Document their nomination as the Green DiSC representative (e.g. include in job description, institutional profile page)
  - Include their contact details in this documentation (e.g. institutional email)
3) Highlight how this is recognised within the organisation (optional)

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That a Green DiSC representative has been nominated and that their role and responsibilities are well-documented within the organisation. 
- That staff members can easily identify who this representative is.
- That you have thought about how this work is being recognised internally.

**Evidence can include:**
- Screenshot of their institutional profile page highlighting their role as Green DiSC representative.
- Screenshot of their role description in group/institutional documentation.
- Updated role description for the staff member. 
- List of ways of how this is being recognised within the team and within the organisation.

## Include digital sustainability in induction procedures for the RCI team

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-general-blue)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RCI)%20G2--B-white)

### Impact
Emphasises the importance of adopting efficient and sustainable practices from day one.

### Description
While there may be a substantial amount of information for new starters, it is crucial that efficient and sustainable computing practices are emphasised on arrival, when new staff behaviour can be most influenced.

### How?
Sustainability induction procedures can be developed by the institution's central sustainability team, or used just for your team and inspired from examples in other institutions. Sustainable computing advice should not be separate but rather fully integrated into induction materials.

This criteria refers to training about digital and computing sustainability, not generic campus sustainability efforts.

*📣 Ask your Central Team! If your central team has applied or is applying to Green DiSC as well, you should be able to ask them about the evidence they provided for this criterion and what relevant information is available for you to include in your application.*

### Checklist
1) Identify suitable induction material applicable to your team
  - Search on your institution’s intranet or contact your central sustainability team if appropriate
  - You can also consult online resources shared on the ESCS forum or listed above. 
2) Include material covering digital sustainability in induction pack
  - Detail how this material is disseminated and how it is made accessible to new starters (including how many have received it already)

### Resources and Case Studies
- [Sustainable Computing in Science course (EMBL-EBI)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/online/courses/sustainable-computing-in-science/)
- [Green Software Practitioner Course](https://learn.greensoftware.foundation)
- [Green HPC System Course (EPCC/Carpentries)](https://epcced.github.io/2025-07-02_GreenHPC_UCL/)

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- What induction material is used with new starters (including where it comes from, e.g. created from scratch or built from existing material).
- How is it being shared, and how many staff members have seen it.

**Evidence can include:**
- Copy of the induction material* (e.g. PDF, docx, pptx)
- Communication (e.g. photos, screenshot, PDF, emails) showing how the induction material is shared with new starters

*You may use discretion to redact any information you deem confidential/sensitive, as long as you are still able to show the relevant evidence for the purpose of fulfilling the action.

## Centralise sustainable computing information in a repository

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-general-blue)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RCI)%20G3--B-white)

### Impact
Ensures green computing resources are easily accessible for regular maintenance and effective dissemination.

### Description
Maintain a repository with sustainable computing-related information relevant to your user community. In addition to RCI users, these are shared with the institution’s central sustainability and IT teams, and with other RCI teams within the institution (if any).

### How?
Set up a repository (e.g. on your department or institution intranet) for sustainable computing resources. The repository should be kept up-to-date and shared with your users.

### Checklist
1) Set up a repository containing sustainable computing resources that are relevant to your team and your user community.
2) Document where the repository is located, what information it contains, and if possible how many have access.

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That there is a central repository created and maintained by the RCI team that holds relevant sustainable computing resources.
- Where the repository is located, what resources it stores, and how many have access (if information is available).
- That this central repository is accessible to the RCI team’s user community, central sustainability team, IT teams, and other RCI teams within the institution (if any).

**Evidence can include:**
- Screenshots of the central repository (e.g. on your intranet).
- Description of the repository location, resources stored, and user access (if available).
- Screenshots showing how the repository is disseminated with users.

## Catalog the office IT equipment

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-offices-yellow)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RCI)%2001--B-white)

### Impact
Enables the team to monitor the equipment owned, ensuring that none gets forgotten about.

### Description
Create an inventory of the different computing hardware used by the RCI team in the office (not personal equipment), whether or not it is currently in use, as well as suppliers and warranty policies that apply. This list is updated yearly and includes laptops, desktop computers, monitors, tablets, external hard drives, and testing equipment (if any). This should not include server rooms’ equipment, as this is a separate criteria.

*Why? Embodied carbon, or the emissions associated with the manufacturing, transportation, installation, maintenance, and end-of-life stages of a product, can take up a significant portion of emissions for equipment. For desktops and laptops, manufacturing takes up about 80% of the total life cycle emissions of the product – greatly outweighing the environmental impacts of the usage phase. Keeping track of equipment is a good way to limit how much new hardware is bought.*

### How?
List the IT equipment owned by the RCI team by linking with colleagues (e.g. circulate a survey). Where data is missing, this should be clearly highlighted so that it can be revisited in the future. In the case where a personal device is used significantly for work (e.g. using your own laptop), then this may be included for monitoring purposes. Unused equipment (e.g. spare or unusable equipment or parts) should also be listed and identified as such. Include information on actions to be taken for each, and information on how to interact with asset management systems, if available.

### Checklist
1) Inventory the relevant computing hardware equipment used by each member of the RCI team
  - Laptops (i.e. brand, model, general specs)
  - External monitors (e.g. size, specs)
  - External hard drives (e.g. SSD, HDD)
2) For each, identify quantity, owner etc.
  - Indicate the quantity per item
  - Indicate the owner
  - Indicate if it’s currently in use
  - (Optional) Indicate the location, if office/storage is spread out

### Resources and Case Studies
You may use [this template](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1GEUPeHO6CnJhCOhJHdSKYH6c0cKhPyXy3UUZzWpv7WI/edit?gid=0#gid=0) created by a previous awardee as reference, just download a copy if you would like to adopt the table(s).

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That the inventory has been put together and covers the entire RCI team.
- That enough data is included in the inventory and gaps are clearly identified.
- That unused hardware is clearly identified.

**Evidence can include:**
- The filled inventory with the different elements listed in the checklist.

## Document the "Reuse, Repair, Recycle" process for IT equipment

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-offices-yellow)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RCI)%2002--B-white)

### Impact
Understand what your team’s and institution's policies are for minimising and recycling e-waste from your team.

### Description
There is documentation of how broken, spare, and unusable equipment (i.e. electronic waste) from the RCI team is dealt with. As much as possible, broken equipment is repaired, spare equipment is reallocated, and any electronic waste is recycled.

*Why? Non-recycled e-waste is a rapidly growing issue across the world, and electronic devices piling up in dump sites – often in low income countries – are responsible for serious health consequences.*

### How?
List the disposing streams available from institution guidelines, as well as any more specific best-practices implemented by your team. These processes can include internal reuse, external reuse, recycling, resale through external vendors, and processing any electronic waste through take-back schemes.

*📣 Ask your Central Team! If your central team has applied or is applying to Green DiSC as well, you should be able to ask them about the evidence they provided for this criterion and what relevant information is available for you to include in your application.*

### Checklist
1) List the different processes in place to Reuse, Repair, Recycle electronic hardware
2) Ensure your team is complying with them and communicate them with your teammates

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That you have a list of the different processes in place to "Reuse, Repair, Recycle" your computing hardware.
- That these have been communicated to your entire team.
- That these processes are followed.

**Evidence can include:**
- Details of the different processes in place, with explanation of how it's implemented in practice in your team.
- Proof of communication of these processes within the team.

## Catalog the major data resources managed by the RCI team

![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RCI)%20DS1--B-white)

### Impact
Allows the team to track the major data resources used and facilitates sustainable data management. 

### Description

This inventory is updated regularly (frequency depends on field, at least yearly).

1) Link with the rest of your group to create an inventory of all the main data resources
For each, add the following elements (if needed, by linking with the IT team):
  - Name
  - Storage location
  - Number of users
  - Size
  - Is the data unique or duplicated from elsewhere
  - Back-up location

## Start a data cleaning process

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-data%20storage-red)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RCI)%20DS3--B-white)

### Impact
Avoids unnecessary accumulation of data that is no longer needed.

### Description
There is a clear and regularly occurring process (twice a year is ideal) for the cleaning of data directories owned by the RCI team. This process should include regular reminders for deletion of temporary/outdated files.

### How?
Organise regular cleaning in coordination with central IT teams and users.

*📣 Ask your Central Team! If your central team has applied or is applying to Green DiSC as well, you should be able to ask them about the evidence they provided for this criterion and what relevant information is available for you to include in your application.*

### Checklist
1) Document the data cleaning process and its frequency
2) Implement it and document when it's happened which repositories were included, who took part, and how much space was freed
  - Include detail on the frequency and next scheduled cleaning.

### References
- [Eleven quick tips for organizing a data cleaning challenge](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013791)

### Resources and Case Studies
- For best practice, you can use [this resource](https://dmponline.dcc.ac.uk) as a DMP template, but there are many other options available online.

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That there is a clear process for data cleaning.
- That it's been implemented, and what its effect was (i.e. describe the type of data cleared and size of storage freed).

**Evidence can include:**
- The data cleaning policy and where it is located.
- Screenshots of communications to organise data cleaning and showing when the reminders are sent out.
- Documentation of effects of the data cleaning event.

## Catalog the computing infrastructures managed by the RCI team

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-compute-silver)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RCI)%20C1--B-white)

### Impact
Provides an exhaustive list of the services managed by the RCI team and the sustainability credentials of each.

### Description
There is a list of the computing infrastructures (i.e. buildings, data centres) managed by the RCI team, including both on-site (e.g. local data centres, servers in the offices) and off-site infrastructures (e.g. remote institutional computing facilities). This inventory is updated regularly (frequency depends on field, at least yearly).

### How?
This inventory should at least include, for each site: location, Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE), cooling technology used, and information on the electricity sources of each infrastructure (i.e. either main power grid, most common, or dedicated power source such as a solar farm). Power purchase agreements can be mentioned, but don’t replace listing the primary source. Noteworthy energy efficiency technology implemented can be included in this inventory. Cloud providers are not included in this scope, as they will not be managed by your team.

### Checklist
1) Create a list of computing infrastructures with appropriate details (listed above), both for on-site and off-site data centres

### How will this be reviewed?

- That on-site data centres have all the information needed about them to understand their environmental impacts. 
- That off-site infrastructures are also listed with as much information as possible.
- That gaps are clearly identified. 

**Evidence can include:**
- The filled inventory with the different elements listed in this Action.

## Catalog servers' hardware

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-compute-silver)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RCI)%20C2--B-white)

### Impact
Allows assessment of the hardware's environmental impact (e.g. manufacturing and end-of-life treatment).

### Description
Create an inventory of the different servers’ hardware, whether or not it is currently in use, as well as suppliers and warranty policies that apply.

*Why? Embodied carbon, or the emissions associated with the manufacturing, transportation, installation, maintenance, and end-of-life stages of a product, can take up a significant portion of emissions for equipment. For servers, this takes up about 20% of the total life cycle emissions of the product. Keeping track of equipment is a good way to limit how much new hardware is bought.*

  - Computing core
  - Storage units
2) For each, identify its usage status, supplier, location, warranty policy etc.

  - Indicate the supplier
  - Indicate the storage location
  - Indicate the warranty policy (if applicable)

### How will this be reviewed?

## Overall energy monitoring of data centres

![Category general](https://img.shields.io/badge/category-compute-silver)
![Criteria ID](https://img.shields.io/badge/Criteria%20ID-(RCI)%20C6--B-white)

### Impact
Enables better resource management, reduces carbon emissions, improves energy efficiency, and supports the transition to more sustainable infrastructures.

### Description
Conduct overall energy monitoring on all the on-site server rooms (i.e. aggregate consumption for the entire room/building that the RCI team controls) and colocation data centre environments on a regular basis. This criterion’s scope does not include remote (e.g. third party, off-site) cloud resources.

### How?
Monitor energy usage across on-site computing infrastructures managed by the RCI team. This can be done through electricity meters in the buildings or by aggregating the measurements of the servers’ power distribution units, for example.

### Checklist
1) Proof that overall energy monitoring is being conducted
  - If available, include how the data is being reported, how it is being used, and if there are targets to reduce or optimise energy usage in the future
2) Monitor granular data of usage across time, if available (optional)

### How will this be reviewed?
**Your submission should demonstrate:**
- That you are regularly monitoring overall energy usage of on-site server rooms and colocation data centre environments.

**Evidence can include:**
- Overall energy usage data.
- If available, further information on how overall energy usage data is reported, used, and if these feed into sustainability targets or similar.
- If available, granular data of energy usage across time.
